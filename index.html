Leaf math

Leaf season goes wrong in three places: the pile judged by how it looks from the window instead of by the liters it actually holds, the tarp trips guessed at two and discovered at twelve, and the bags bought by optimism instead of by division. Leaf math turns the lawn into the liters, turns the liters into the trips, and turns the pile into the bags. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

Open the calculators

The three tools

  • Pile plan - the lawn area and the leaf depth become the liters of leaves waiting out there.
  • Trip plan - the liters at a labeled tarp load become the trips to the pile or the curb.
  • Bag plan - the liters at a labeled bag size become the bags to buy and fill.

Labeled norms

Labeled norms: 10 L of leaves per m2 per cm of depth, a 200 L tarp load and a 120 L paper bag - all illustrative defaults you can change by measuring. Wet leaves weigh and pack far more than dry ones, loose piles compress to a fraction in the bag, and raking is real work in bursts: pace it, and rake with the wind, not against it. This is planning arithmetic, not a landscaper's quote.
